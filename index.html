// 01 · Before 5.18

South Korea Before the Uprising

5.18 이전 대한민국의 상황

To understand why Gwangju erupted, you need to understand what came before it.

// Korean Political History
1948–1960
🏛️ Syngman Rhee — First President. Authoritarian rule. Ended by the April 19 Student Revolution (4.19혁명).
1961–1979
⚔️ Park Chung-hee — Military coup. 18 years of authoritarian rule. Rapid economic growth, but no political freedom. Assassinated by his own intelligence chief on Oct 26, 1979.
Dec 12, 1979
🔴 12.12 Military Coup (하극상 쿠데타) — General Chun Doo-hwan seizes control of the military in an internal coup. The "Seoul Spring" — a brief period of democratic hope — is crushed.
Spring 1980
📢 The Seoul Spring (서울의 봄) — Nationwide pro-democracy protests. Students and citizens demand a new constitution and end to martial law. Chun responds by expanding martial law nationwide on May 17.

After 18 years under Park Chung-hee's authoritarian rule, South Koreans finally saw a window for democracy open in late 1979. Park's assassination created a power vacuum — and citizens across the country took to the streets, demanding free elections and a democratic constitution. This brief moment of hope became known as the "Seoul Spring" (서울의 봄).

박정희 18년 독재 이후, 1979년 그의 암살로 권력 공백이 생기며 민주화의 가능성이 열렸습니다. 전국 각지에서 민주주의를 요구하는 시위가 일었고, 이를 '서울의 봄'이라 부릅니다.

General Chun Doo-hwan had other plans. On December 12, 1979, he staged an internal military coup — arresting the army chief of staff and seizing control of the armed forces. By May 1980, as pro-democracy protests grew louder, Chun extended martial law to the entire country on May 17, closed all universities, sealed the National Assembly, and arrested opposition leaders including Kim Dae-jung. The Seoul Spring was over. What came next was Gwangju.

전두환은 1979년 12월 12일 군사 쿠데타를 일으켜 군권을 장악했습니다. 1980년 5월 17일, 전국 비상계엄을 선포하고 대학을 폐쇄, 국회를 봉쇄, 민주화 인사를 체포했습니다. 서울의 봄은 끝났고, 다음은 광주였습니다.

// 02 · The Uprising

The May 18th Uprising

5.18 민주화운동 — 1980년 5월 18일–27일

Ten days that changed South Korea forever. The streets we walk today are where this happened.

"It all started when machine-gun fire ripped through the heart of the city. A young man fell right next to me. He writhed and groaned. When the gunfire ceased, a few of us bystanders carried him to a hospital. Someone howled, 'Enough is enough!' Almost naturally, we sought to arm ourselves."
"기관총 사격이 도심을 찢고 지나갔을 때 모든 것이 시작되었다. 바로 내 옆에서 젊은 남자가 쓰러졌다. 총성이 멈추자 우리 몇몇이 그를 병원으로 옮겼다. 누군가 울부짖었다, '이제 그만이다!' 우리는 자연스럽게 무기를 찾게 되었다."
— Eyewitness testimony · Gwangju Diary: Beyond Death, Beyond the Darkness of the Age (광주일기), Lee Jae-eui, 1985

May 18, morning. Students gathered at the gate of Chonnam National University to protest its closure. By 9:30 a.m., approximately 200 students faced 30 paratroopers. The soldiers charged. The protest moved downtown to Geumnam-ro. By the afternoon, 686 soldiers from elite airborne units arrived — and what followed was indiscriminate brutality. Clubs, bayonets, beatings. Not just students, but ordinary citizens, mothers, and children.

5월 18일 오전, 전남대 정문 앞에 학생 약 200명이 모였습니다. 공수부대가 돌격하며 시위는 금남로로 번졌습니다. 오후에는 정예 공수부대 686명이 추가 투입됐습니다.

May 21. Soldiers opened fire on civilians in front of the Provincial Office. Dozens killed on the spot. Outraged citizens raided police stations and reserve army armories for weapons. Women textile workers seized hundreds of rifles. Coalminers provided dynamite. The citizens of Gwangju pushed the military out of the city.

5월 21일, 도청 앞에서 계엄군이 집단 발포했습니다. 수십 명이 현장에서 사망했습니다. 시민들은 무기를 확보해 시민군을 결성, 계엄군을 광주 외곽으로 몰아냈습니다.

May 22–26 — Liberated Gwangju. With no soldiers, citizens ran the city themselves. Street vendors and housewives prepared and distributed food on the streets. Blood drives were organized spontaneously. Wealthy citizens fled; ordinary people stayed and shared everything they had.

5월 22~26일, 계엄군 없는 광주에서 시민들이 스스로 도시를 운영했습니다. 거리에서 음식을 나누고, 자발적으로 헌혈하고, 치안을 유지했습니다.

"You guys have to tell others exactly what you witnessed today. Be witnesses to history of how we fought and died."
"여러분은 오늘 목격한 것을 다른 사람들에게 정확히 전해야 합니다. 우리가 어떻게 싸우고 죽었는지 역사의 증인이 되어 주십시오."
— Yoon Sang-won (윤상원), Spokesman of the Citizens' Army · spoken to students on the night of May 26, 1980, hours before the final assault · Gwangju News archive

Dawn, May 27. Troops stormed the Provincial Office. The last defenders — who had refused to leave, believing history would remember them — were mostly killed or captured. The ten-day uprising was over. Between May 17 and late July, 2,699 people were arrested in connection with the uprising. Many were tortured.

5월 27일 새벽, 계엄군이 전남도청을 진입했습니다. 끝까지 도청을 지키던 항전대원들 대부분이 사망하거나 체포됐습니다. 이후 5월 17일~7월 사이 총 2,699명이 연행됐습니다.

// 03 · After 5.18

The Road to Korean Democracy

5.18 이후 — 한국 민주주의의 여정

5.18 didn't end in 1980. Its spirit became the engine of South Korea's democratic movement.

// The Long Road to Truth & Democracy
1980–87
🔇 Suppression — Chun's regime brands 5.18 a "communist riot." The word "5.18" is forbidden. Underground pamphlets and banned testimonies circulate secretly — the truth kept alive at great personal risk.
Jun 1987
🔥 June Democratic Uprising (6월 민주항쟁) — Nationwide pro-democracy protests force Chun's government to accept direct presidential elections. 5.18's spirit was the driving force behind this movement.
1988
🏛️ Parliamentary Hearings — The first public airing of what really happened in Gwangju. Survivors testify. The "communist riot" narrative begins to collapse.
1995
⚖️ 5.18 Special Act — Chun Doo-hwan and Roh Tae-woo are prosecuted and convicted. Chun receives a death sentence (later commuted).
1997
🕊️ National Memorial Day — May 18 is officially designated as a national day of commemoration.
2002
🌿 National Cemetery — National May 18th Democracy Cemetery opened. Victims are laid to rest with full national honors.
2011
🌐 UNESCO Memory of the World — The 5.18 archives are inscribed as a UNESCO Memory of the World. The documents held in the Archive we visit today are part of this listing.
2017
🚁 Helicopter Gunfire Confirmed — The Ministry of Defense officially confirms that military helicopters fired on civilians — a fact denied for 37 years. The 245 bullet holes in Jeonil Building are the physical evidence.

5.18 did not end in defeat. The underground testimonies, the banned pamphlets, the whispered truth about Gwangju became the fuel for South Korea's democracy movement. When the June 1987 Uprising finally forced free elections, it was built on the foundation of what happened in Gwangju seven years earlier. Today's South Korean democracy was paid for, in part, on these streets.

5.18은 패배로 끝나지 않았습니다. 광주의 진실은 지하에서 전해지며 1987년 6월 민주항쟁의 토대가 됐습니다. 오늘 대한민국의 민주주의는 이 거리에서 지불된 대가 위에 서 있습니다.

// 04 · Recommended Reading

Human Acts

소년이 온다 · Han Kang · 2014

A novel set on the very streets we walk today. The author was born in Gwangju in 1970 — she was nine years old when the uprising happened.

🏆
Han Kang wins the 2024 Nobel Prize in Literature
On October 10, 2024, Han Kang became the first South Korean writer and the first Asian woman to receive the Nobel Prize in Literature. The Swedish Academy cited her "intense poetic prose that confronts historical traumas and exposes the fragility of human life." Human Acts — the novel set in Gwangju — is considered central to that recognition.
Source: nobelprize.org, Oct 10 2024
소년이
온다
Human Acts
한강 Han Kang
Han Kang 한강 창비, 2014 🏆 2024 Nobel Prize

Gwangju, May 1980. Fifteen-year-old Dong-ho goes to the Provincial Office looking for a missing friend — and ends up helping collect the bodies of the dead. Over ten days, and then across the decades that follow, the novel bears witness through six different voices.

1980년 5월 광주. 열다섯 살 소년 동호는 친구를 찾으러 전남도청으로 갔다가 시신 수습을 돕게 됩니다. 소설은 여섯 명의 목소리로 5.18을 증언합니다.

Han Kang asks: "Is it true that human beings are fundamentally cruel?" Through the story of people who tried to protect each other in the face of extreme violence, she etches the meaning of dignity into every page.

한강은 묻습니다 — "인간이란 근본적으로 잔인한 존재인가?" 극한의 폭력 앞에서도 서로를 지키려 했던 광주 시민들의 이야기.

"It wasn't as though we didn't know how overwhelmingly the army outnumbered us. But the strange thing was, it didn't matter. Ever since the uprising began, I'd felt something coursing through me, as overwhelming as an army. Conscience. Conscience, the most terrifying thing in the world."
"우리가 군대에 압도적으로 열세라는 것을 몰랐던 게 아니다. 하지만 이상하게도, 그건 중요하지 않았다. 항쟁이 시작된 이후 나는 무언가가 군대만큼이나 압도적으로 내 안을 흐르고 있음을 느꼈다. 양심. 양심, 세상에서 가장 무서운 것."
— Human Acts (소년이 온다), Han Kang · 창비 2014 · verified via LitCharts / published text

// 05 · Today's Route

Five Historic Sites

오늘의 방문지 — Walking the Memory

What to look for — and think about — at each place we visit today.

01
Geumnam-ro · 금남로
🔥 Heart of the Uprising · 광주 민주화운동의 심장부

The heart of the May 18th Uprising. On May 20th, over 100 taxis and buses lined up on this street, turning their headlights on to lead the citizen protest against military brutality. It was also here that foreign journalists, like Germany's Jürgen Hinzpeter, risked their lives to film the truth, smuggling the footage out to expose the atrocities to the world. On May 21st, troops opened fire on civilians on this exact street. Now preserved as a symbol of the democracy movement. The annual May Democracy Festival is held here every year.

5.18의 심장부. 1980년 5월 20일, 100여 대의 택시와 버스가 금남로에 집결해 전조등을 켜고 계엄군의 과잉 진압에 맞서 차량 시위를 이끌었습니다. 또한, 독일의 위르겐 힌츠페터와 같은 외신기자들이 목숨을 걸고 참상을 카메라에 담아 전 세계에 광주의 진실을 알린 현장입니다. 5월 21일에는 이 거리에서 시민들을 향한 집단 발포가 이루어졌습니다. 현재는 민주화운동 상징 거리로, 매년 5월 민주화운동 대축제가 열립니다.

💭 Think about this
Tens of thousands of people stood on this wide road. When the gunfire rang out — where did they run? What does it feel like to walk where they stood?
이 넓은 도로 위에 수만 명이 서 있었다. 총성이 울렸을 때, 사람들은 어디로 달아났을까.
🔴 Site of Mass Shooting🎉 Annual May Festival
02
Former Provincial Office · 옛 전남도청
⚔️ The Last Stand · 최후 항전 · Human Acts backdrop

The site of the final stand, dawn of May 27th. When soldiers told them to evacuate, the last defenders refused — believing: "If we die here, history will remember us." Now preserved alongside the National Asian Culture Complex (ACC). The gymnasium inside is where novel character Dong-ho collected bodies in Human Acts.

5월 27일 새벽 최후 항전의 장소. 국립아시아문화전당(ACC)과 연계 보존 중. 소설 «소년이 온다»에서 동호가 시신을 수습했던 상무관이 이 건물 안에 있었습니다.

📖 Human Acts · Chapter 1
In Chapter 1 of Human Acts, Dong-ho waits in this gymnasium for his friend's body. The entire first chapter is set here — in this building.
소설 1장 전체가 이 상무관 체육관을 배경으로 한다.
⚔️ Final Stand📖 Human ActsACC
03
May 18th Archive · 5.18 민주화운동 기록관
🌐 UNESCO Memory of the World · 세계기록유산

Originally the Catholic Center. In May 1980, a priest and staff on floors 6–7 witnessed soldiers cracking down on crowds below on Geumnam-ro — and documented it all in photographs and records. Those documents became decisive evidence of the truth. UNESCO Memory of the World since 2011.

원래 옛 가톨릭센터. 1980년 5월, 6·7층에서 금남로의 진압 장면을 목격하고 사진과 기록으로 남겼습니다. 그 기록들이 역사적 진실의 결정적 증거가 됐습니다. 2011년 유네스코 세계기록유산.

✍️ Remember
The people who took those photographs risked their lives to do so. Documenting the truth was itself an act of resistance.
당시 사진을 찍은 사람들은 목숨을 걸었다. 기록하는 행위 자체가 저항이었다.
🌐 UNESCO Memory of the WorldCatholic Center
04
Jeonil Building 245 · 전일빌딩 245
🚁 245 Bullet Holes from Helicopter Gunfire · 헬기 기총 사격 흔적

The 245 in the building's name is the count of bullet holes left by helicopter gunfire. Denied for 37 years — officially confirmed by the Ministry of Defense only in 2017. Exhibitions on floors 7–9. Helicopter attack animation plays on a regular schedule. VR experience available.

건물 이름 245는 헬기 기총 사격 탄흔의 수입니다. 37년간 부인됐다가 2017년 국방부 조사로 공식 확인됐습니다. 7·8·9층 전시관, 기총 사격 애니메이션, VR 체험 가능.

🔍 The Number 245
245 bullet holes on one building. From the 7th-floor cafe terrace, looking down at Geumnam-ro, you can imagine what the gunners saw from the helicopter above in 1980.
한 건물에 245개의 총탄 자국. 7층 카페 테라스에서 금남로를 내려다보면 헬기에서의 시야가 느껴진다.
🚁 245 Bullet Holes7–9F ExhibitionVR☕ 7F Cafe
05
National May 18th Democracy Cemetery · 국립 5.18 민주묘지
🕊️ Resting place of those who died · 희생자들이 잠든 곳

The resting place of those who died, established as a national cemetery in 2002. Each gravestone carries a name, an age, and the circumstances of death. Twenty years old. Sixteen. Fifty. Every May 18th, the national memorial ceremony is held here and "March for the Beloved" (임을 위한 행진곡) fills the air.

항쟁 희생자들이 잠든 국립묘지 (2002년 조성). 묘비 하나하나에 이름과 나이, 희생 경위가 새겨져 있습니다. 매년 5월 18일 국가 기념식에서 «임을 위한 행진곡»이 울려 퍼집니다.

🕊️ At This Place
Please read the names and ages on the gravestones. The moment you see them as people — not statistics — history becomes real.
묘비의 이름과 나이를 직접 읽어보길. 숫자가 아닌 사람으로 마주하는 순간, 역사가 현실이 된다.

공룡알빵, 나비파이 — 광주 빵집의 전설. 방문자 리뷰 7,737건. 중소벤처기업부 인증 백년가게.

5.18 Through the Lens

스크린으로 만나는 5.18 이야기

Essential movies and dramas that help you visualize and emotionalize the history we explored today.

🎬 Movie (2017)
A Taxi Driver · 택시운전사
The true story of a Seoul taxi driver and the German journalist Jürgen Hinzpeter who snuck into Gwangju to capture the uprising on film. Crucial for understanding the role of media in the movement.
서울의 택시운전사가 독일 외신기자 위르겐 힌츠페터를 태우고 광주로 들어가 참상을 목격하고 이를 세계에 알린 실화 기반 영화.
🎬 Movie (2007)
May 18 · 화려한 휴가
A dramatic film focusing on the ordinary citizens—brothers, lovers, and neighbors—who had no choice but to form a citizen's army (Shimingun) to protect their families on Geumnam-ro.
금남로에서 가족을 지키기 위해 자발적으로 시민군이 될 수밖에 없었던 평범한 소시민들의 시선에서 10일간의 항쟁을 그려낸 영화.
📺 Drama (2021)
Youth of May · 오월의 청춘
A retro romance drama depicting the fateful love story of a medical student and a nurse. It beautifully yet painfully portrays how the historical whirlwind of May 1980 swept through the lives of young individuals.
1980년 5월이라는 역사의 소용돌이 속에서 운명처럼 서로에게 빠져버린 의대생과 간호사의 애틋한 사랑을 담은 휴먼 멜로드라마.
🎬 Movie (1999)
Peppermint Candy · 박하사탕
A masterpiece that traces back the life of a ruined man. It chillingly shows how the trauma of being deployed as a martial law soldier in Gwangju in May 1980 shatters an innocent young man's soul over twenty years.
한 남자의 인생을 역순으로 추적하며, 1980년 5월 계엄군으로 투입되었던 트라우마가 어떻게 순수했던 청년의 영혼을 파괴했는지 보여주는 명작.
